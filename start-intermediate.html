The plateau

It's still Thursday. Here's Thursday's workout.

You're not the guy who disappears for six months.

You train four days a week. You log your sets. You track your protein. You've read enough programme theory to argue about it.

And your bench has been sitting at the same number since last spring.

You get it for five. Next week you get it for six. You try adding 2.5kg, grind out four ugly reps, drop back down, and tell yourself the extra rep was progress.

Then you scroll back through your log and see the same number, over and over, for a year.

The photo never matches the mirror. Your shoulder's been off for a month. The rack you needed was taken on Tuesday so you did something else. Last week was a write-off because work was insane.

None of that shows up anywhere. The plan doesn't know. The plan doesn't care.

It wasn't your effort

The effort was never the problem.

You've been through the list.

Not recovering enough. Too much volume. Not enough volume. Need to cut. Need to bulk. Technique's off. CNS is fried. Bad genetics.

And then the one you don't say out loud — maybe I'm not actually working as hard as I think I am.

That one sticks, because you know you are.

Here's what's actually true. You've done the hard part. You showed up for four years without needing to be motivated. You logged everything. You ate the protein.

The problem is what happened to the effort afterwards.

Your log already knows

Your log wasn't a training record. It was a diary.

Every session you've trained has produced the exact information needed to program the next one.

The weight that moved easily. The set where the bar slowed halfway. The rep that turned into a five-second death march. The exercise you skipped because the cable station was busy. The week you slept four hours a night.

All of it is sitting in your log right now.

Nothing has ever read it.

Your app asked you ten questions when you signed up, generated a plan, and then stopped paying attention. You could have your worst session in two years and it'd say nice work — here's Thursday.

That programme you bought was written before it knew anything about you. You could send it to twenty guys in your gym and nobody would notice.

Your trainer reviewed you monthly, from a check-in form you half-filled on the train.

ChatGPT has no memory of last Tuesday and no rules about what it's allowed to prescribe. Which is why it hands people nine-day weeks and thirty sets to failure with total confidence.

And you decide mid-session. Tired, mid-set, phone in hand, defaulting to whatever you did last time.

That last one is the plateau. Mechanically. That's what it looks like from the inside.

What actually breaks it

Every session. Into the next session.

Not a better programme. You've had four.

Not more discipline. You've got more than most people in your gym.

The only thing that breaks a year-long plateau is a system that reads what you actually did in the last session and writes the next one from it.

That's the whole thing. Everything else is a diary with a nicer interface.

The FORGE loop

This is what FORGE does. Four steps.

1
It asks what nothing else asks.
Not just goal and experience. Your sport. The days you can actually train. The equipment you genuinely have. The shoulder you're working around. Under two minutes.
2
You log every set as you lift.
Weight, reps, how it moved.
3
It reads that session before it writes the next one.
Not weekly. Not at signup. Session to session.
4
Next session changes.
Sets, reps and target effort move with what your last session actually looked like. Not with what a spreadsheet decided in advance.
Then it goes again. That's the loop. That's the product.
How you know it's reading

Four things a static plan structurally cannot do.

It backs you off when the data says to.

You grind out your top set. Ugly. Last rep took five seconds.

A normal app never asks, and Thursday looks exactly the same either way.

FORGE asks how the session went, then drops the target — fewer sets, or a lower RPE.

Your first reaction is what? I can do that. Then you do it, get clean reps instead of a death march, and it builds you back from there.

That is not the app going easy on you. That is the first time something looked at what happened.

It calls the deload before you hit the wall.

Not when you are wrecked. When your last few sessions have flattened out across several lifts and you have not noticed the pattern — because you are inside it, and your instinct is always do another session and see if it improves.

It swaps the exercise when the rack is taken.

Or when your shoulder is off. Or when you have got 35 minutes instead of an hour. Tell it, and it gives you something that does the same job with the equipment you have actually got. Most apps assume the gym exists in a vacuum.

It tells you why it changed something.

Weekly review, in plain English. Not a dashboard of numbers you have to interpret yourself. The reasoning, so you can disagree with it if you want.

No, it's not that AI

FORGE isn't a chatbot with a dumbbell icon.

You've asked ChatGPT to write you a programme.

You know what comes back. A nine-day week. Thirty sets to failure. Confident, detailed, and structurally wrong — with no idea it's wrong.

It is constrained by rules that do not bend. Every movement comes from a fixed exercise list, filtered to the equipment you actually have, sized to the days and the session length you gave it. And what it is allowed to change after a session is limited in code, not in a prompt.

And more importantly, it isn't guessing. It has your training history.

The AI isn't the interesting part. The interesting part is that you log what happened and it changes what comes next.

I get why people think that. I've used ChatGPT for training myself. Asking an AI to give you a workout isn't coaching. It'll give you one — then what? It doesn't know what you did last week. It doesn't track your progression. It doesn't know you're exhausted from going to failure on everything. The AI isn't the product. The system around it is.

— Kevin, founder

You play five-a-side on Wednesdays. Your left shoulder has been unhappy since February. You can train Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday — except when you can't.

And it keeps building around it when things change.

That's not a feature list. That's the difference between a plan you follow for three weeks and one you're still on in month six.

Common questions

The things people ask before they sign up.

"Every app says it's personalised."
They do. And most of them mean a decision tree that picks a template at signup and never looks again. The test isn't what an app says at signup — it's whether anything changes after a bad session. Log three sessions in FORGE and watch what the fourth one looks like. That's a checkable claim, which is why we're happy to make it.
"I'll use it for two weeks and stop."
Probably true of the last four apps you downloaded, because nothing about them changed. What keeps people here is that the plan responds — miss a week and it adjusts rather than pretending nothing happened. You don't restart. You pick up.
"AI can't program training."
Raw AI can't. It has no memory and no rules. FORGE has both — your full history, and hard constraints on what it can prescribe. Different thing.
"I've got a programme from the wiki that's fine."
The wiki programmes are good. That's not the problem. The problem is that a wiki programme can't see that your bench moved badly on Tuesday, and you're the one making the call about Thursday — tired, mid-session, defaulting to last time.
"What about my sport / my injury?"
It asks at onboarding and programmes around both. If your shoulder's off, tell it mid-session and it swaps the movement.
"What happens to my training data?"
It's yours. Used to write your programme, nothing else. Export it or delete it whenever you want.
"It's a web app, not in the App Store."
Add it to your home screen and it behaves like any other app. No install, no store, no 200MB download. Works on iPhone and Android.
