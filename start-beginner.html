The actual problem

You're the only one guessing.

You walk in. You do the machines you recognise. Maybe some treadmill. Some curls, because you know what curls are.

You spend more time watching what everyone else is doing than actually training.

You've got eight exercises saved in your Notes app and a folder of TikToks you've never opened twice. You don't know what order they go in. You don't know what weight you're supposed to use.

So you pick whatever's free and hope it's roughly right.

Then you go two weeks without going, because what's the point if you don't know if any of it's working.

Everyone else in there looks like they know exactly what they're doing.

You're not bad at this

That's what it looks like when nobody ever told you what to do.

Somewhere in the last year you decided you're just not built for it.

You watched someone load up a bar and thought yeah, that's not me. You used the same 10kg dumbbells for a month and never asked whether you should be on 12.5, because you had no idea how you'd know.

That's not weakness. That's not bad genetics.

Nobody is naturally good at the gym. Every single person in there who looks like they know what they're doing was taught, followed something, or spent years working it out badly.

You just went straight to the working-it-out-badly part.

Why the free stuff didn't work

You needed someone to tell you what to do today.

It's not that the information was wrong. It's that it wasn't instructions.

A saved workout tells you 3 sets of 10. With what weight? How long do you rest? What if you can't do ten? What if ten's easy — do you go up next time, and by how much?

None of that's there. So you follow it for a week and quietly stop.

A YouTube video teaches you one exercise. It doesn't tell you what to do on Tuesday.

ChatGPT hands you a huge list with loads of exercises and no idea what you actually do when you walk in the door.

A free app gives you a plan on day one and then never changes it. Have a terrible session, come back destroyed, and it says the same thing it was always going to say.

Every one of those assumes you already know the things you're trying to find out.

You didn't need more information.

I paid CHF 360 once for a coaching programme that promised exactly that. It arrived about a minute after I signed up. There's no version of that where someone actually looked at my situation in sixty seconds. That's not personal. That's a template with my name typed onto it.

— Kevin, founder
What actually works

You need something that knows what happened last time.

Two things, and they have to come together.

Something that tells you exactly what to do when you walk in — which exercise, how many sets, what weight, what to do if it's too easy or too hard.

And something that changes it based on how it actually went.

That second part is what everything free is missing. A list can't know that leg day wrecked you for three days. A PDF can't know you couldn't work out how to use the machine so you skipped it.

How FORGE works

Four steps.

1
It asks where you're actually at.
Not a test. It asks what you've done, what equipment you've got, what days you can train, and what you want. No wrong answers. Under two minutes.
2
It gives you a plan you can actually do.
Not a six-day bodybuilding split. Something that fits your week and starts where you are.
3
You log what you did.
Tap the weight and reps. Say if something was too hard, too easy, or you couldn't do it.
4
Next session changes.
The target moves up when you are ready. It eases off when you overdid it. It swaps exercises you could not do.
Then it goes again.
The first week

What that looks like in week one.

When you overdo it, it notices.

First couple of leg sessions leave you barely able to walk. Not injured — just wrecked. Tell it. The next session doesn't smash your legs again and tell you to push through. It pulls back.

When you can't do something, you say so.

My gym didn't have a hack squat once. FORGE swapped it for something that did the same job — I didn't have to stand there guessing what to do instead. Another time, a hamstring curl machine was giving me discomfort. I told it, and it moved me to Romanian deadlifts instead. The discomfort went.

— Kevin, founder

That's a small thing that isn't small. With a normal programme, the answer when something doesn't work for you is: well, that's what it says, do it anyway.

It remembers.

You don't explain yourself twice. Tell it once that you can't do something and it stays gone.

It tells you when you've done enough.

There's a thing beginners get stuck on — if you're not completely destroyed, you assume you did it wrong. FORGE will tell you no, that was the right amount, go home.

No judgement

It won't make you feel stupid.

Fair warning about the AI thing: you're probably wondering how software knows what someone who's never trained properly should be doing.

Two answers.

It asks first. Nothing gets prescribed before it knows what you've actually done and what you've got access to.

And it's built on training rules, not guesswork. It can't hand you the thirty-set nightmare programme that ChatGPT will confidently give anyone who asks. There are limits on what it's allowed to prescribe you, and those limits are the point.

You can ask it anything. What the exercise is. Whether you're doing it right. Whether the weight's too light. Nobody's watching, nobody's judging, and it won't assume you already know.

It starts where you are

You don't need to be ready. You need to start.

Three days a week. Two, if that's what's realistic. Thirty-five minutes on the days you're pushed.

Just machines, if the free weights area still feels like too much. It'll get you there when you're ready — and moving there is a step it plans, not a leap you make on your own.

Common questions

The things people ask before they sign up.

"I'm too much of a beginner for this."
That's who it's built for. It asks what you've done before anything gets written, and it starts there. There's no level you need to reach first.
"Will it give me something ridiculous?"
No. It's built on hard limits about what's safe and recoverable for someone at your stage. The programme you get in week one will look almost too easy. That's deliberate.
"I don't know how to do the exercises."
Ask it anything, any time, and it will explain without assuming you already know. Forge adds a video demonstration on every exercise.
"I'll stop using it like everything else."
Most beginners quit because they don't know if it's working, not because they lost interest. You'll see your numbers move week to week, which is the thing that's been missing.
"What if I miss a week?"
It adjusts and carries on. You don't restart, and nothing tells you off.
"What if I've got barely any equipment?"
